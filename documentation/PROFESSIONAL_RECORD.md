## 1. Coding class for lawyers: Africa Legal Innovation Week 2022 (Lawyers Hub)

**Role:** designed and presented the class.
**Title:** *Coding Class: Creating a Landing Page for Your Law Firm* (37
slides, with a "coding class tools" folder of practice files).
**Content:**
- Why lawyers should understand code: handling programming-related disputes
  (including their own) and preparing a defence with an understanding of
  the back end.
- Proprietary, personal and open-source programs and their licences.
- Practical 1: HTML tags, attributes and page structure; then the legal
  implications and risks of claiming authorship.
- Copyright in code: automatic once recorded in a tangible medium;
  protects expression, not ideas; rights to reproduce, issue copies, modify,
  license and sell; voluntary registration with the Kenya Copyright Board
  (KECOBO) recommended; use of the © notice.
- Practical 2: a law-firm landing page from a template (head, header and
  hero, footer, then CSS).

**Article:** "Why I taught lawyers to write code".

## 2. AI & Digital Trade Policy Hackathon: Africa Law Tech Festival 2023

**Role:** in charge of the hackathon (justice innovation team); also built
the hackathon site (*DigitalTrade.Africa* / *ALTF2023-Hackathon*).
**Festival theme:** *Digital Trade in Africa: The AfCFTA and the Single
Digital Market*, Nairobi.
**Method:** user-driven, deliberative, inclusive, participatory co-creation.
**Themes:** trade facilitation; market intelligence; digital platforms;
policy and regulation.
**Objectives:** collaboration across developers, designers, entrepreneurs
and subject experts; AI for AfCFTA implementation; solutions that are AI
driven, improve market access, streamline trade and drive integration;
capacity building; sustainable AI businesses.
**Two tracks:** *policy*: close the data gap on digital trade, outline an
AI governance framework, identify policy considerations for a single digital
market; *tech*: an AI-enabled observatory (database) of AfCFTA challenges
and opportunities.
**Timeline:** briefing (virtual) Fri 7 July 2023; launch Tue 11 July after
the opening addresses; submissions and pre-recorded pitch videos (Sparkhire)
by noon Wed 12 July, judging and awards that day.
**Prizes:** KSh 50,000 / 30,000 / 20,000.

## 3. Data protection: trained on, and helped deliver, ADPI material

**Role:** contributed to and was trained using the Lawyers Hub / Africa
Digital Policy Institute slides *Data Protection Law: The Act* (Kenya's
Data Protection Act); supported delivery of the Africa Data Protection
Course and CIPP/E training.
**Covered:** the Act's purpose (Article 31(c)–(d) of the Constitution);
key definitions (consent as express, unequivocal, free, specific and
informed; personal and sensitive personal data, including health status;
controller; processor; profiling); the Office of the Data Protection
Commissioner, its functions and powers; registration of controllers and
processors (certificates valid 24 months); data protection officers;
principles and obligations; sensitive data; transfers outside Kenya;
exemptions; enforcement (complaints concluded within 90 days, enforcement
and penalty notices, administrative fines up to KSh 5M or 1% of turnover,
appeal to the High Court).

**Article:** "Privacy is a form of respect".

## 4. Research: data collection and research initiatives

- **Boda-Boda Law Project:** co-organised field data collection in Kisumu
  and Namanga; contributed to the published report; coordinated trainings
  for riders. (Catalog §6.5.)
- **State of Digital Identification and AI in Africa (Survey & Sprint),
  2023:** facilitated data collection. Small sample (about 15 respondents on
  most questions). Headline results:
  - 14 of 15 believe continental digital ID can improve access to services
    and integration; 95% rate regional initiatives such as the AfCFTA very
    important; 100% say oversight bodies need financial and operational
    independence.
  - 0% think the Malabo Convention has been effective (44% no, 56% unsure);
    45.5% say their country's digital ID transition is still in planning.
  - Challenges spread evenly: interoperability, connectivity, cross-border
    privacy, limited cooperation; 22.6% chose "all of them".
  - On LLMs in digital ID: 50% somewhat improved / 50% unsure; 90% had seen
    no positive use case; top concern data breaches (54.6%); top hoped-for
    benefit, access for people with limited literacy or language (60%).
  - Top law-reform priority: inclusion and accessibility for marginalised
    populations (44.4%), then data protection and privacy (33.3%).

**Article:** "What a small survey taught me about digital identity".
